## Hero of Alexandria's Automata (~1st century CE) (0070-01)

- **id**: `hero-of-alexandria-automata-1c`
- **corpus**: academic
- **creator**: Hero (Heron) of Alexandria (Roman Egypt, ~1st century CE)
- **disclosure**: Hero (Heron) of Alexandria. 'Pneumatica' and 'Automata' (Περὶ αὐτοματοποιητικῆς), ~1st century CE (c. 60-70 CE). Alexandria, Roman Egypt. Earliest surviving treatises on automaton-building.
- **ip status**: public-domain
- **prior art notes**: Hero of Alexandria's Automata (~1st century CE) are the earliest surviving treatises on automaton-building. 1,950+-year-deep public-domain prior art.

## Banu Musa's Book of Ingenious Devices (~850 CE; the al-Jazari predecessor) (0850-01)

- **id**: `banu-musa-ingenious-devices-850`
- **corpus**: academic
- **creator**: Banu Musa brothers (Muhammad + Ahmad + al-Hasan ibn Musa ibn Shakir); Baghdad House of Wisdom, ~850 CE
- **disclosure**: Banu Musa brothers (Muhammad, Ahmad, and al-Hasan ibn Musa ibn Shakir). 'Kitab al-Hiyal' ('The Book of Ingenious Devices'). ~850 CE (compiled under the patronage of the Abbasid caliph al-Ma'mun). Baghdad, House of Wisdom (Bayt al-Hikma). ~100 devices described.
- **ip status**: public-domain
- **prior art notes**: Banu Musa's Book of Ingenious Devices (Baghdad House of Wisdom ~850 CE) is the foundational Islamic-Golden-Age automata + mechanism treatise — the predecessor of al-Jazari (corpus al-jazari-ingenious-devices-1206). 1,175-year-deep public-domain prior art. Built on Hero of Alexandria (corpus); the programmable flute player prefigures pinned-cylinder programming + the Jacquard loom (corpus).

## Leonardo da Vinci's Mechanical Knight (~1495) (1495-01)

- **creator**: Leonardo da Vinci (Milan, ~1495)
- **disclosure**: Leonardo da Vinci. Mechanical Knight designed ~1495 (sketches in the Codex Atlanticus). Built for a pageant at the court of Ludovico Sforza, Milan. Reconstructed by Mark Rosheim (2002) from the surviving sketches.

- **prior art notes**: Leonardo da Vinci's Mechanical Knight (~1495 Milan) is the first known humanoid robot design in Western history. 530-year-deep public-domain prior art.

- **corpus**: academic

- **disclosure**: Japanese karakuri-shi (automaton-makers), Edo period (~1603-1868). Documented in Hosokawa Hanzō Yorinao's 'Karakuri Zui' (機巧図彙, 'Illustrated Collection of Mechanisms'), 1796 — the world's first published book of mechanical engineering by a non-Western author.

- **prior art notes**: Karakuri Ningyō (Edo-period Japan 17th-19th c.; 'Karakuri Zui' 1796) is Japan's foundational automaton tradition. 350+-year-deep public-domain prior art. The cultural ancestor of Japanese robotics.

## Vaucanson's Digesting Duck + Flute Player (1738-1739) (1738-01)

- **id**: `vaucanson-digesting-duck-1739`
- **corpus**: academic
- **creator**: Jacques de Vaucanson (Paris, 1737-1745)
- **disclosure**: Jacques de Vaucanson. The Flute Player (1737, exhibited 1738), the Tambourine Player (1738), and the Digesting Duck (Canard Digérateur, 1739). Paris, France. Vaucanson later became Inspector of Silk Manufactures and built an automated loom (1745) that influenced Jacquard.
- **ip status**: public-domain
- **prior art notes**: Vaucanson's Digesting Duck + Flute Player (Paris 1737-1739; automated loom 1745) are the Enlightenment's most famous automata + the bridge to the Jacquard loom. 286-year-deep public-domain prior art.

## The Mechanical Turk (von Kempelen 1770; chess-playing automaton) (1770-01)

- **id**: `von-kempelen-mechanical-turk-1770`
- **corpus**: academic
- **creator**: Wolfgang von Kempelen (Pressburg/Vienna, 1770); later Johann Nepomuk Mälzel
- **disclosure**: Wolfgang von Kempelen. The Turk (Schachtürke / Automaton Chess Player), first exhibited 1770 at the court of Maria Theresa, Vienna. Later owned by Johann Nepomuk Mälzel. Destroyed in a fire 1854. Famously revealed as a hoax — a hidden human chess master operated it.
- **ip status**: public-domain
- **prior art notes**: The Mechanical Turk (von Kempelen Vienna 1770-1854) is the most famous fraudulent automaton + the namesake of crowdsourced human computation. 255-year-deep public-domain prior art. The enduring cautionary tale of 'autonomous' systems secretly requiring hidden human operators — directly relevant to teleoperation-disclosure ethics.

## Maillardet's Automaton (~1800; drawing-and-writing machine) (1800-01)

- **id**: `maillardet-automaton-1800`
- **corpus**: academic
- **creator**: Henri Maillardet (Swiss-British, ~1800)
- **disclosure**: Henri Maillardet (Swiss mechanician working in London). The Draughtsman-Writer automaton, built ~1800. Now at the Franklin Institute, Philadelphia (donated 1928 after a fire revealed it; restored).
- **ip status**: public-domain
- **prior art notes**: Maillardet's Automaton (~1800 London) has the largest cam-based 'memory' of any automaton of its era. 225-year-deep public-domain prior art. Conceptual ancestor of stored-program machines.

## Jacquard Loom (Joseph Marie Jacquard 1804; punch-card programmable machine) (1804-01)

- **id**: `jacquard-loom-1804`
- **corpus**: academic
- **creator**: Joseph Marie Jacquard (Lyon, France, 1804); building on Bouchon (1725) + Falcon (1728) + Vaucanson (1745)
- **disclosure**: Joseph Marie Jacquard. The Jacquard machine (an attachment for the drawloom), patented 1804 in Lyon, France. Built on earlier punch-card / perforated-cylinder loom mechanisms by Basile Bouchon (1725), Jean-Baptiste Falcon (1728), and Jacques de Vaucanson (1745, corpus).
- **ip status**: public-domain
- **prior art notes**: The Jacquard Loom (Joseph Marie Jacquard Lyon 1804) is the first practical large-scale programmable machine. 221-year-deep public-domain prior art. The conceptual ancestor of all programmable automation + stored-program computation.

## Olimpia (E.T.A. Hoffmann's 'The Sandman'; 1816) (1816-01)

- **id**: `olimpia-hoffmann-sandman-1816`
- **corpus**: fictional
- **creator**: E.T.A. Hoffmann ('Der Sandmann', Berlin, 1816)
- **disclosure**: Hoffmann, E.T.A. 'Der Sandmann' ('The Sandman'). In 'Die Nachtstücke' ('Night Pieces'), 1816. Berlin. Adapted as the ballet 'Coppélia' (Léo Delibes, 1870) and Act I of the opera 'The Tales of Hoffmann' (Offenbach, 1881). Sigmund Freud's essay 'The Uncanny' (1919) analyzes it at length.

- **prior art notes**: Olimpia (E.T.A. Hoffmann's 'The Sandman' 1816) is the literary automaton that gave us 'the uncanny valley' a century before the term — and the template for the deceptively-human android. 209-year-deep public-domain prior art. Literary ancestor of the Maschinenmensch (corpus), Ex Machina's Ava (corpus), Westworld hosts (corpus), replicants (corpus). Relevant to the uncanny-valley problem in humanoid design (Ameca/Sophia/Mentee corpus).

## Antikythera Mechanism (~150-100 BCE; ancient analog computer) (2006-11)

- **id**: `antikythera-mechanism-150bce`
- **corpus**: academic
- **creator**: Hellenistic Greek artisan(s) (~150-100 BCE; possibly Rhodes / Hipparchus school)
- **disclosure**: Built ~150-100 BCE (Hellenistic Greece, possibly Rhodes). Recovered 1901 from a shipwreck off Antikythera island. First detailed analysis: Freeth, T. et al. 'Decoding the ancient Greek astronomical calculator known as the Antikythera Mechanism'. Nature 444:587-591, November 2006.
- **ip status**: public-domain
- **prior art notes**: The Antikythera Mechanism (~150-100 BCE Hellenistic Greece) is the oldest known geared analog computer. 2,000+-year-deep public-domain prior art. The conceptual ancestor of all geared mechanism + clockwork automation.
