## The Golem of Prague (Jewish folklore; the artificial servant of clay) (1808-01)

- **id**: `golem-of-prague-folklore`
- **corpus**: fictional
- **creator**: Jewish folklore (golem traditions from the Talmud onward; Prague legend attributed to the Maharal, popularized 19th-20th c.)
- **disclosure**: Jewish folklore; the 'Golem of Prague' legend attributed to Rabbi Judah Loew ben Bezalel (the Maharal of Prague, c. 1525-1609). The legend in its familiar form was popularized in the 19th-early 20th century (Jacob Grimm 1808; Berthold Auerbach 1837; Yudl Rosenberg's 'Nifla'os Maharal' 1909; Gustav Meyrink's novel 'Der Golem' 1915; the 1920 German film 'Der Golem'). Older golem traditions trace to the Talmud + Sefer Yetzirah + medieval Kabbalah.
- **ip status**: public-domain (folklore)
- **prior art notes**: The Golem of Prague (Jewish folklore; Prague legend attributed to the Maharal, popularized 19th-20th c.) is the ur-myth of the artificial humanoid servant + the original 'creation turns on its creator' cautionary tale. Centuries-deep public-domain prior art (predates the word 'robot' by centuries). Foundational template for Frankenstein (corpus), R.U.R. robots (corpus), Maschinenmensch (corpus). The 'word activates/deactivates' mechanism is the original kill-switch / corrigibility problem.
